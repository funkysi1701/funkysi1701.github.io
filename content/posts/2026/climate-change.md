It has been a hot summer, with quite a few heatwaves pushing past 30°C. I am not an environmental campaigner, but the weather is increasingly making me think we need to take better care of our planet. This post is about the levers I already have: who I bank with, who supplies my energy, who represents me in Parliament, and the company I work for.

There is a film called the [National Emergency Briefing](https://www.nebriefing.org/) that is being screened in churches and community venues. It covers the issues far better than I can — check their site for a screening near you.

[Our Health Our Planet](https://ourhealthourplanet.co.uk/public-action-impact) suggests a few practical areas to concentrate on. I used that list as a checklist against my own life.

## Banking

The first item is switching to a bank that does not invest in fossil fuels. I bank with [Monzo](/tools-and-resources/#monzo-bank), so I looked at how they stack up.

**What looks good**

- Monzo says it does not invest customer deposits in fossil-fuel-based energy companies, arms companies, or tobacco companies.
- Its offices use 100% renewable electricity.
- 93% of cards issued in FY2025 were made using recycled PVC, with solvent-free ink.
- It has committed to a net-zero target for 2030 and publishes its carbon footprint annually.

**Where they fall short**

- A 28% increase in total emissions between FY2025 and FY2026, mostly from the supply chain rather than offices.
- Net-zero progress is behind target.
- It does not actively finance green projects.

**My take:** Monzo is still a better fit for me than a high-street bank that funds fossil fuels with customer deposits. I am staying for now, but I will keep an eye on whether that net-zero plan catches up. If you are still with a traditional bank, moving is one of the higher-impact switches on the Our Health Our Planet list.

## Energy supplier

I use [Octopus Energy](/tools-and-resources/#octopus-energy) for gas and electricity.

**What looks good**

- Green electricity tariffs backed by renewable generation.
- Substantial involvement in wind and solar, including investment through its generation business.
- Support for flexibility on the grid, which helps absorb intermittent renewables.

In June 2026, Octopus Energy Generation announced £8.5 billion in renewable energy assets under management, 5.7 GW of projects across more than 400 sites in 21 countries, and £930 million deployed into clean energy in six months.

**Where they fall short**

Octopus is still a major gas supplier. Its 2026 environmental reporting shows that gas accounts for roughly 92% of its overall corporate carbon footprint. That is the hard bit for any dual-fuel supplier while UK homes still rely on gas boilers.

**My take:** I am happy to stay with Octopus on the electricity side, and their generation story is one of the reasons. The gas number is a reminder that supplier choice is not the whole answer — heat pumps, insulation, and when we use power matter too. I have written before about [charting usage with their API](/posts/2021/charting-my-energy-usage-with-the-octopus-energy-api/), which is a useful way to see where the kilowatt-hours actually go.

## My MP

Our Health Our Planet also says we need to speak up and talk to our MP about climate action. Mine is [Lee Pitcher](https://www.voteclimate.uk/mps/lee-pitcher), Labour MP for Doncaster East and the Isle of Axholme. To see how your own MP votes on green issues, use [Vote Climate](https://voteclimate.uk).

### Examples of votes supporting environmental action

| Date | Parliamentary vote |
| -------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Sep 2024 | Great British Energy Bill |
| Oct 2024 | Great British Energy Bill, third reading |
| Jan 2025 | Clean Heat Market Mechanism |
| Apr 2025 | Onshore wind and solar planning |
| Feb 2026 | Greenhouse Gas Emissions Trading Scheme |
| Jun 2026 | Carbon budget / Climate Change Act orders |

Vote Climate currently rates him Good, with 16 of 21 climate-related votes in favour (3 against, 2 did not vote). That is a solid record, not a perfect one. Writing a short letter or sending a message about the issues you care about still matters — MPs notice when constituents raise climate alongside cost of living and local services.

## At work

I work at [Microlise](https://www.microlise.com/). Their technology helps transport companies:

- Reduce unnecessary vehicle mileage through route optimisation.
- Cut fuel use through better driving behaviour and less engine idling.
- Improve vehicle utilisation and maintenance, extending useful life.
- Support the move to electric commercial vehicles.

Microlise reports that its technology helped UK customers save 246 million litres of fuel over five years — more than 645 million kg of CO₂. It received the London Stock Exchange Green Economy Mark for the sixth consecutive year in 2026, with 60.9% of revenue classified as green in that assessment.

That is the company side. As a developer, the same habit of asking “what waste are we creating?” applies to software too: right-sizing cloud resources, turning off idle environments, choosing efficient defaults, and measuring before we scale. You do not need to work in telematics to make those choices in your own stack.

## Future actions

Here is what I am taking from this exercise:

1. Stay with Monzo for now, but check their next annual carbon report against the 2030 net-zero path.
2. Keep Octopus for electricity, and dig further into how we heat the house and when we use power.
3. Message Lee Pitcher about the climate issues that matter locally, and invite you to do the same with your MP via [Vote Climate](https://voteclimate.uk).
4. Keep looking for a [National Emergency Briefing](https://www.nebriefing.org/) screening nearby.
5. At work, keep treating efficiency as a feature — fewer wasted miles on the road, fewer wasted cycles in the cloud.

If you have already switched bank or supplier, written to your MP, or found a practical change that stuck, I would love to hear what worked in the comments.
